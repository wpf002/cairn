# CAIRN — COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS

Sourced from the four tiers supplied, August 2026. Every listed URL was pulled; where a page
was unreachable (`thinkorange.com` redirect loop, `help.orangeapps.church` expired cert,
`apps.apple.com/mx/.../id6751195473` 404) the same facts were recovered from the canonical
listing or search. Figures are as displayed on the store/research pages at time of pull.

## PART 1 — WHAT WAS FOUND

### TIER 1 — Parent Cue (the only direct competitor)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Publisher | The reThink Group / **Orange**, now trading as **Amazing Life** (merged with Amazing Life Foundation Jan 2025, CEO Chris Heaslip). Founded by Reggie Joiner, ex-Family Ministry Director, North Point |
| iOS | 4.8 ★ (2.4K ratings), 63 MB, Lifestyle, 4+, v9.0.10, EN/ES, iOS 13+, Mac M1, visionOS |
| Android | **3.8 ★ (1.3K reviews)**, 100K+ installs, updated Apr 10 2026 |
| Price | Free + in-app "Phase Content" subscription (monthly or annual, via store) |
| Scale | 500K+ lifetime users · 1M+ children · **34,000+ partner churches, 70+ denominations** · content in ES, DE, PT, FR, Mandarin, NL |

**Product model.** The whole app hangs off one number: **936 weeks** from birth to 18. Four
"cues" per child per week. A monthly faith-and-character topic, supported by weekly Bible-story
videos, a monthly memory verse, and daily devotionals — all resolved by the child's age.
Multi-child. Printable/shareable PDFs. A **church finder** that syncs the family to a local
ministry and then pipes that ministry's events and notifications into the app.

**Paid tier.** Unlocks Phase Content, drawn from *The Phase Project* (Orange's developmental
research programme, book: *It's Just a Phase: Why Every Life Stage of a Kid Matters*): weekly
phase cues, monthly resources and downloads, and a year-long overview of the child's phase.
Phase also underwrites Orange's church curricula (First Look / 252 Kids / XP3 MS / XP3 HS) and
sells physical **Phase Guides** through a store.

**Documented weaknesses.**
- Android at 3.8 against iOS at 4.8 — the gap is entirely engineering: login/password-reset
  failure, immediate crash-on-open surviving reinstall and cache clear, video starting
  mid-stream, photo orientation bugs, profile pictures not editable after creation.
- Entitlement failures: a June 2025 iOS review rates the free app 5/5 and the paid subscription
  1/5 for not granting access after payment.
- Tone fatigue — "could use more variation in tone and theme… the theme song is almost as long
  as each segment."

**Structural limits.** Birth → 18 only. No pregnancy. Nothing past 18. No medical layer. No AI.
No mother/father differentiation. No provenance on any claim. Content is downstream of a
*church curriculum*, so it is aligned to what the kids' ministry taught on Sunday rather than to
what this child needs developmentally — which is the seam Cairn is built in.

### TIER 2 — Christian parenting apps

**Parently** (Verselingo Communications, `id6738328536` / `bzn.christian.teen.parenting`)
- 4.6 ★ (44 reviews), **10K+ installs**, updated Dec 2024. Free + **$11.90/yr or $23.99 lifetime**.
  Health & Fitness on iOS, Parenting on Play. Declares **no data collected**.
- Features: per-child notes/journal with personalized scriptures and prayers; curated Christian
  parenting articles; **weekly reflection exercises**; child profiles; special-date reminders.
- Review: *"not too happy about needing to purchase most of the content."*
- Strategically interesting: it is a portfolio play. The same publisher ships 12+ Christian
  apps — Sermon Notes, Christian Journal, TwoAsOne (marriage), Gracify (meditation), and a
  **separate Christian Pregnancy Tracker**. Nobody in that portfolio has connected pregnancy to
  parenting. That is the exact bridge Cairn is built on, unbuilt, by someone already in the market.

**Bible Widget: Parenting Guide** (Camille Labs / Camille Holdings Pty, `id6755133230` /
`com.aussielabs.betterparent`)
- 4.5 ★ (2 ratings), **100+ installs**, updated Jul 2026. **$9.99/mo, $49.99/yr — and it runs ads.**
  129.9 MB, Education, iPad-first.
- Five modules: Child Behaviour & Discipline · Emotional & Spiritual Growth · Parenting With
  Purpose · Family Connection · Faith in Everyday Moments. Bite-sized lessons, "Biblical insight
  blended with modern parenting science," "grace-filled, not guilt-driven."
- Delivery surface is a **home-screen widget** with daily verses and tips.
- Reviews: lessons fail to load; content overlaps between modules.
- Read: a thin, high-priced, ad-supported land-grab on the same positioning language Cairn uses.
  The positioning is *not* defensible by claim — only by depth.

**TruPlay Games** — adjacent, not competing. 35+ games, comics and videos for ages 4–12 in one
app; the World of Rhym original universe; Book of God and The Action Bible content. **Up to 10
child profiles on one family subscription**, 7-day free trial, **zero ads, zero chatrooms, zero
IAP**, **kidSAFE Seal certified**, endorsed by the Billy Graham Evangelistic Association and
Focus on the Family. Worth studying purely as a trust playbook: third-party child-safety
certification plus institutional endorsement is how you get a Christian parent to hand over a
card.

**faith.tools/for-parents — the category map (59 apps).** The finding is fragmentation. Every
app is a single-purpose tool and none owns the longitudinal spine:

| Cluster | Examples |
|---|---|
| Kids' streaming | Yippee TV, minno, BRAVE+, Bentkey, MyTRIBE.watch, Listener Kids, Bible Kids |
| Filtering / accountability | Covenant Eyes, Bark, Canopy, Accountable2You, Qustodio, Kidslox, Safe Surfer, Tech Lockdown, Gertrude, LivingRoom, VidAngel, ClearPlay, Verso, LeadMeNot |
| Safe hardware | Gabb Wireless, Gabb Music, Pinwheel |
| Family devotional | Lectio for Families, New City Catechism, UCB Spark, Bible Engagement Project (free, AG), Bible Memory Kids, Instill |
| Teen-culture guidance | **Axis** — parent guides and conversation kits on culture and faith |
| Christian AI | **Sanctuary** (Bible study, prayer, *parenting decisions*, Kids mode 8–16), Virggil, TheoScope, Glorifind |
| Screen-time / rest | clearspace, Sabbath Space, Limit Phone |
| Ministry infrastructure | Safehold, Foster the City, Focus on the Family app |

Nearest threats in this list: **Axis** (owns the teen conversation-guide niche Cairn's GUIDE
THEM section targets, and already has church distribution), **Sanctuary** (a Christian AI
already answering parenting questions, with no developmental spine underneath it), and
**Bible Engagement Project** (free, age-aligned discipleship curriculum backed by a denomination).

### TIER 3 — Secular developmental apps (where the craft is)

**Kinedu** — pregnancy → age 6. **9M families, 5M+ installs.** iOS 4.6 ★ (7K); **Play 3.2 ★
(42.4K)**. Free tier: baby tracker, 750+ expert articles, milestones. Premium $7–12.99/mo,
$39.99–79.99/yr.
- 3,000+ step-by-step **video** activities; personalized daily plan by age or pregnancy phase;
  **Stanford partnership** on activity design; **progress reports across four developmental
  domains, explicitly modelled on what pediatricians use**; live and recorded expert classes;
  1:1 coaching; **"Ana," an AI assistant with unlimited questions on premium**; up to 5 babies;
  unlimited account sharing; Siri voice logging for sleep; AI-powered search; dark mode.
- Awards: Harvard Center on the Developing Child, Open IDEO, MIT Solve, Dubai Cares.
- **The 3.2 Android rating is the most useful data point in this entire research pass.** Causes,
  verbatim: milestone tracking was free and got moved behind the paywall, *retroactively erasing
  access to logged history*; a 7-day-trial popup on every launch with no visible exit; three
  marketing emails within two hours of install; "20 different things to click on"; the app
  taking a full minute to scroll. **Contains ads.** Collects location and shares data for
  third-party advertising.

**Huckleberry** — 4.9 ★ (72K), Apple **Editors' Choice**, #25 in Medical, 5M+ families, 179
countries.
- Free: contraction timer, one-touch logging (sleep, diaper, feed, pump, growth, milestones,
  weight, potty, meds, activities), bilateral breastfeeding timer, multi-child, **multi-caregiver
  sync**, reminders.
- **Plus $11.99/mo, $58.99/yr**: SweetSpot® (predicts the ideal wake window and nap time),
  Schedule Creator, Insights, trend reports, **AI logging by text, voice or photo**.
- **Premium $14.99/mo, $119.99/yr**: **"Berry" — 24/7 AI parenting guidance** and custom
  expert-designed sleep plans with human check-ins. Plus one-off Sleep Improvement Plan $49.99.
- watchOS, **Live Activities**, share reports with the pediatrician. Explicitly gentle and
  evidence-based, no cry-it-out. Claims 93% of families report improved sleep.
- The one critique worth heeding: *"I obsess over when I forget to log something."* Tracking
  creates guilt. Cairn's formation framework can create the same guilt if scored badly.

**The Wonder Weeks** — 4.8 ★ (**122K ratings**), 16 languages. Free + $1.99–4.99/mo, $9.99/3mo,
$34.99/24mo, and eBook chapters at $0.99.
- 10 mental leaps in 20 months. **A personalized leap calendar that notifies you *before* a leap
  starts**, "signals" to recognize one in progress, 77 playtime games, ~400 trackable skills,
  diary, **partner app linking**, community forum, videos, polls. Redesign rolling out end of 2026.
- Critiques: "too complicated and redundant," interactive features "feel like homework
  assignments," too much parent data entry during the hardest weeks.

**Tovi's positioning against Kinedu** — worth reading as a competitive brief written against a
market leader: activity libraries go *repetitive*, they are *not personalized*, and they *do not
touch family coordination*. Tovi's answer: personalized guidance birth→12 plus family
scheduling. Also mapped: BabySparks (0–3, $6/mo, curriculum structure), Pathways (0–5,
**completely free**, research-backed milestone checklists with video demos), Lovevery (physical
kits from $36 + free app), Khan Academy Kids.

**Apple's "Apps for Parents" editorial room (21 apps).** Relevant entries: **Good Inside** —
"24/7 Parenting Support" (Dr. Becky; the secular Help Me Parent This, already funded and
already famous); **Tinybeans** — private shared family photo calendar (the nearest thing to
Cairn's Parenting Journey); **Cozi** (shared family calendar); Napper, Solid Starts, Peanut,
Life360, Fayr (co-parenting). Also a reminder that App Store editorial placement is a live
acquisition channel for a native-only product.

### TIER 4 — Faith app platforms (the money and the distribution playbook)

**Hallow** — **$105M raised** (Series C $50M led by Goodwater; Series B $40M led by Peter Thiel
and Drive Capital). ~40 staff as of 2021. Growth: 1.5M downloads Dec 2021 → 7M Apr 2023 → 10M
May 2023 → **#1 overall on the App Store, Feb 2024, the first religious app ever to do it** →
600M+ prayers completed by Aug 2024. 150 countries, 7 languages. **~40% of users are not Catholic.**
- **Pricing: $9.99/mo · $69.99/yr · Family $119.99/yr for 6 · students $2.50/mo · 50% off Catholic
  educators · parish packages.** One donated subscription for every paid one.
- 10,000+ sessions, 50+ guides, selectable 5/10/15-minute lengths, prayer playlists by emotional
  state (stress, anxiety, joy, humility, discernment), **seasonal community challenges (Pray40 for
  Lent, Pray25 for Advent)**, **Hallow Family** shared prayer intentions, journaling after every
  session, sleep Bible stories, Bible tab with bookmarks/highlights/notes, offline download,
  prayer reminders, **Apple Health mindfulness integration**.
- Distribution: celebrity guides (Wahlberg, Neeson, Jonathon Roumie of *The Chosen*), Fr. Mike
  Schmitz's *Bible in a Year*, a **Super Bowl ad**, and systematic **parish / diocese / school**
  institutional sales from day one.
- Named risks: political controversy (the Neeson partnership drew backlash over his abortion
  stance); Christian identification in wealthy markets projected to fall 78% → 66% by 2050.

**Glorify** — **$80.1–84M raised** (a16z, SoftBank LatAm, Thiel Fellowship, Hillhouse, plus Jason
Derulo, Michael Ovitz, Corey Gamble). Founded 2020, Henry Costa, Witney UK. **20–25M+ users**,
4.9 ★ (94K). $9.99/mo, ~$59.99/yr, occasional lifetime.
- Daily five-minute devotional on a fixed spine: short passage → pastoral reflection → one or two
  journal prompts → closing prayer, narrated or silent, paired with a worship track. Worship and
  ambient library. 10–30 minute Scripture sleep meditations. Guided journal with gratitude log,
  prayer-request log, saved highlights.
- **Rated the best UX in the entire Christian app category** — calm colours, generous whitespace,
  soft animation, and deliberately **no streaks, no guilt notifications, no badge counters.**
- **May 2026: merged with Confidein and shipped the "Glorify Ring" — the first Christian smart
  ring — and reports tripled revenue YoY.**
- Weaknesses: not a study tool (no commentary, cross-references, original languages, or full
  Bible reader); evangelical-charismatic defaults; phone and tablet only.

**Pray.com** — 17–18M users, 100M+ podcast downloads, 4.8 ★ (191K), $34M raised, founded 2016,
iOS + Android + **web**. $9.99/mo, $69.99/yr.
- Bedtime Bible Stories narrated by James Earl Jones and Phil Wickham, produced like audiobooks;
  Daily Prayer anchored by a named pastor; **family devotionals — couples, kids, mealtimes,
  Advent/Lent/Easter — integrated into the main app rather than walled into a kids' app**; Bible
  in a Year; long-form sleep Psalms.
- Weaknesses: the home screen reads as a **streaming carousel with no "do this next"**; the
  paywall lands on exactly the family/bedtime content parents came for, within the first week.

**Market context.** Faith-app venture funding: **$6.1M (2016) → $48.5M (2020) → $175.3M (2021).**
Category price anchor is settled at **$9.99/mo and $59.99–69.99/yr**.

## PART 2 — WHAT THEY DO THAT CAIRN'S ROADMAP DOES NOT

Ranked by how much it would cost Cairn to be missing it.

| # | Gap | Who does it | Why it matters to Cairn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Institutional / church distribution** | Parent Cue (34,000 churches, church finder + sync + ministry notifications), Hallow (parish, diocese, school packages; 50%-off educator pricing), Axis, Bible Engagement Project | §31 currently says acquisition is "App Store search plus whatever off-platform channels exist." That is not a channel, it is the absence of one. Every profitable player in this category acquires through congregations. A solo-operated app paying App Store CAC against Hallow's Super Bowl budget loses. **This is the single largest omission in the roadmap.** |
| **2** | **A finite-time counter as the brand** | Parent Cue: "936 weeks" | It is simultaneously the name, the hook, the notification, the widget and the reason to open. Cairn has a 21-year roadmap *visual* (§22) but no counter. Cairn's number is larger and better: **~1,092 weeks** to 21, or "you have 4,847 days left in your house." |
| **3** | **OS surface integration** | Bible Widget (home-screen widget is the whole product), Huckleberry (watchOS, Live Activities), Kinedu (Siri logging), Hallow (Apple Health mindfulness) | Cairn ships zero widgets, zero watch, zero Siri, and explicitly **defers Apple Health to Phase 8**. For a native-only product (§31), OS surfaces are the compensation for having no web presence. A TODAY widget is cheap and it is the highest-frequency impression the app will ever get. |
| **4** | **Seasonal community challenges** | Hallow's Pray40 / Pray25 — the highest-retention mechanic in the faith category | Cairn defers "community" wholesale to Phase 6+ on moderation grounds. That reasoning is right for user-generated content and **wrong for a family challenge**, which has no UGC and therefore no moderation cost. A "40 Days of Blessing Your Child" Lent challenge is a Phase 4 feature, not a Phase 8 one. |
| **5** | **Multi-caregiver, not just spouse** | Huckleberry (caregiver sync), Brightwheel (multi-role on one child), Wonder Weeks (partner linking), TruPlay (10 profiles, one family sub) | Cairn's permission edges exist but only two adult roles are designed for. §14's letter-writing ceremony **requires grandparents, mentors and trusted adults** — the feature is specified and the access model to support it is not. |
| **6** | **Shareable artifacts** | Parent Cue (printable + shareable PDFs), Glorify (shareable verse cards) | §33a's client-side encryption makes sharing structurally hard, which is correct for child data and fatal for organic growth. Cairn needs a deliberate, explicitly non-sensitive share path — a devotional card, a conversation guide, a ceremony invitation — or it has **zero viral surface**. |
| **7** | **Named human experts** | Kinedu (live classes, 1:1 coaching), Huckleberry (expert sleep plans with check-ins), Hallow (Fr. Mike Schmitz, Bishop Barron, Scott Hahn), Pray.com (Pastor Mike Novotny), Glorify (named teachers) | Cairn's §19 provenance chain has a `reviewer` field and no face. In a trust category, **named humans are the trust**. A recognisable OB, a recognisable pediatrician, a recognisable pastor-theologian on the Tobias gate converts better than any schema. |
| **8** | **Anticipatory intelligence** | Huckleberry SweetSpot® (predicts the wake window), Wonder Weeks (notifies you *before* a leap begins) | §15 promises the future-facing direction and the phase plan builds only the present-tense TODAY. The `opportunity` signal in §21 is already the right primitive — **windows close** — and nothing in the roadmap converts it into a forward-looking notification engine. |
| **9** | **A daily-return mechanic after birth** | Huckleberry, Kinedu, Wonder Weeks, Napper, Glow — all use logging | Cairn ships kick counter, contraction timer, weight and symptom trackers in Phase 3 — and then **drops every one of them at birth**. The roadmap correctly identifies the "birth cliff" that kills pregnancy-only apps, then reproduces it in reverse: after week 40 there is no daily reason to open the app except reading. |
| **10** | **Progress reporting** | Kinedu (reports across 4 developmental domains, modelled on pediatric reports) | Cairn defines **ten** domains (§7) and measures none. Parents want to see movement. The seven-category × stage grid is already a coverage surface — render it. |
| **11** | **Spanish, at minimum** | Parent Cue (EN/ES), Hallow (7 languages), Kinedu (3), Wonder Weeks (16) | Cairn is implicitly English-only with no localization anywhere in Phases 0–8. US Hispanic Christian families are a core buyer for exactly this product. |
| **12** | **A genuinely usable free tier** | Glorify and Pray.com are both praised for it; Kinedu's **3.2** is what the opposite earns | Cairn's §30 free tier paywalls **multiple child profiles** — which punishes precisely the largest, most committed families. Kinedu's cautionary tale is specifically about paywalling something users already had. Move the multi-child gate; gate depth, not family size. |
| **13** | **A physical artifact** | Glorify Ring, Lovevery kits, Orange's printed Phase Guides and store | §14's ceremonies are begging for a printed keepsake — a letter book, a milestone card set. Not Phase 1, but it is a revenue line and a gift-purchase vector nobody has taken in the Christian parenting space. |
| **14** | **Any web surface at all** | Pray.com (web), Kinedu (app.kinedu.com), every competitor's marketing site | §31 locks native-only, which is fine for the *product*. But there is no marketing site anywhere in Phases 0–8, which means no SEO, no shareable landing page, and no link to put in a church bulletin. |
| **15** | **Third-party trust certification** | TruPlay (kidSAFE Seal + BGEA and Focus on the Family endorsements) | Cairn holds more child data than TruPlay and has stronger actual protections (§33a). It currently plans to claim none of it externally. |

## PART 3 — WHAT NOBODY DOES, THAT CAIRN COULD OWN

| # | Opening | Evidence it is genuinely unoccupied |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Conception → 21 as one unbroken record** | Every competitor breaks at a cliff: Wonder Weeks at 20 months, Huckleberry at toddler, Kinedu at 6, TruPlay at 12, Parent Cue at 18, Tovi at 12. **Nobody covers 18–21 at all.** Nobody crosses the birth transition except The Bump's photo album. Orange's own framework stops at 18 despite its source worksheet running to 21. |
| **2** | **The parent as a tracked subject** | Hallow, Glorify and Pray.com form the adult and know nothing about the children. Kinedu, Huckleberry and Wonder Weeks track the child and ignore the parent. §26 (Becoming The Parent They Need) sits in the empty quadrant, and it is voiced — the father's anger and the mother's anxiety are different content. **No product in any tier does this.** |
| **3** | **Mother / father differentiated voice** | Zero occurrences across all 25 sources. Wonder Weeks has *partner linking* — shared access to identical content. §16b is not shared access; it is two genuinely different readings of the same child. It is also the only honest justification for a two-seat price. |
| **4** | **Teaching parents how *not* to use a verse** | Nothing in the Christian tier does exegesis at all, let alone stated misuse. Bible Widget's whole value proposition is "bite-sized content rooted in scripture" with no context layer. The `misuse` field (§16a) is the most credible trust signal available in this category and it costs nothing to market. |
| **5** | **Visible, tappable provenance** | Forbes: only **28%** of pregnancy apps cite scholarly sources. None of the Christian apps cite anything. Cairn's §19 chain is currently a CI gate — surface it in the UI as "why we say this," and it becomes uncopyable, because it is an editorial cost rather than a feature. |
| **6** | **Client-side encryption of child data** | Kinedu collects location and shares for third-party advertising. Huckleberry links health data to identity. Parent Cue collects location. Bible Widget runs ads and says data "can't be deleted." **Nobody encrypts.** "We are cryptographically unable to read your child's file" is both true and marketable. |
| **7** | **Engineering rites of passage** | Robert Lewis's *Raising a Modern-Day Knight* is a book with no software. §14's ceremony flow — mentors and grandparents writing letters, culminating in the age-21 Recognize ceremony — exists nowhere in any app. It is the emotional centre of the product and nothing is remotely near it. |
| **8** | **A formation coverage map** | Every competitor's home screen is a feed or a carousel (Pray.com is explicitly criticised for this: no "do this next"). Nobody renders *what you have and haven't done with this child*. Seven categories × fourteen stages is a natural completion surface — but see the Huckleberry logging-guilt warning: show coverage, never a score. |
| **9** | **A 21-year compounding artifact** | Tinybeans is the nearest analogue and it is an album with no narrative. The Bump's bump-photo-album is one year. "The Story Of Your Childhood," generated client-side at 21 from two parents' threads, is the only retention argument in the entire category that gets *stronger* with tenure. |
| **10** | **Single-parent dignity as a designed feature** | Every faith parenting product silently assumes two parents; Parent Cue's source worksheet is titled "The Smart **Dad**." §16b's `soloVariant` and the Tobias dignity check address a large, underserved and currently mildly insulted segment. |
| **11** | **Closing-window notifications** | Wonder Weeks predicts a leap. Nobody says "you have fourteen months left in the window where this lands." The ▲/● two-signal model (§21) is the only data structure in the category that can express it. |
| **12** | **Cross-voice pairing** | No product lets each parent see what the *other* parent is carrying. A father needs the mother-voiced puberty material to avoid undercutting it. This is the feature that makes the spouse tier worth paying for, rather than just a second login. |
| **13** | **Pregnancy → parenting as one subscription** | Verselingo ships a Christian parenting app *and* a separate Christian pregnancy tracker and has never joined them. The whole category treats these as different products for different people. They are the same person, eight months apart. |

## PART 4 — RISKS THE RESEARCH SURFACES

**Price parity without scale parity.** §30 puts Cairn at $6.99–9.99/mo and $59–79/yr — precisely
level with Hallow ($69.99), Pray.com ($69.99), Glorify ($59.99) and Kinedu ($79.99). Those four
amortise content across 5M–25M users. Cairn's substrate is 14 stages × 7 categories × 2 voices
× solo variants, plus 40 pregnancy weeks, every normative unit carrying exegesis, application,
stated misuse and dual medical + theological sign-off. **Cost per subscriber is an order of
magnitude worse than any comparable.** Annual-first is right; the number may need to be higher,
or the church channel has to carry acquisition. This is a Warren question and it is currently
unanswered.

**Parent Cue is free.** The free tier cannot look like a worse Parent Cue. What Cairn's free tier
gives away has to be something Parent Cue structurally cannot: the pregnancy half, the voice
split, or the provenance.

**Phase 2 is the project.** The MVP scope (pregnancy 4–40 plus ages 0–5) is roughly 44 stage-units
× 7 categories × 2 voices ≈ **600+ fully-warranted normative units before a single screen
renders**, each requiring passages, exegesis, application, misuse, theological reviewer and date,
plus evidence refs on every descriptive unit. Every downstream phase waits on it. The roadmap
already names it the longest pole; the research confirms it is longer than that — Orange spent
"hundreds of hours" and a research programme and a book to produce a shallower framework.

**Engineering quality is a moat here.** Parent Cue sits at 3.8 on Android because of crashes and
login failures. Kinedu sits at 3.2 because of dark-pattern monetisation. WTE ships crashes. The
Bump ships clutter. **In this category, an app that simply works and does not manipulate is
already differentiated** — and that is the cheapest advantage on this list.

## PART 5 — RECOMMENDED ROADMAP AMENDMENTS

1. **Add a church partner programme** as a first-class workstream, landing with Phase 7
   (monetization): church finder, congregation sync, group licensing, and a leader-facing view.
   Nothing else on this list changes the business as much.
2. **Adopt the counter.** ~1,092 weeks to 21. Name it, ship it on the TODAY screen, and put it in
   a home-screen widget in Phase 4.
3. **Pull family challenges forward** from Phase 8 to Phase 4. No UGC, no moderation, highest
   proven retention mechanic in the faith category.
4. **Add OS surfaces to Phase 4**: TODAY widget, watch complication, Siri shortcut for journaling.
   Move Apple Health out of "deferred."
5. **Design a post-birth daily-return mechanic** into Phase 4 so the trackers built in Phase 3
   have a successor. Formation coverage, one-tap "we did this," or a nightly prayer log — not a
   score.
6. **Convert `opportunity` into a notification engine** in Phase 4. Closing windows are the only
   push notification this product can send that a parent will thank it for.
7. **Move the multi-child paywall.** Gate depth, not family size. Kinedu's 3.2 is what the other
   choice costs.
8. **Extend the permission model** beyond spouse to grandparent / mentor / godparent, in Phase 1
   where the edges are already being built, so the §14 ceremony flow has an access model to land on.
9. **Design one shareable, non-sensitive artifact** in Phase 4 — the app's only organic growth
   surface under §33a.
10. **Name the reviewers publicly.** DOC and TOBIAS need faces on a marketing surface, not just
    strings in a provenance record.
11. **Put Spanish in the plan** with a target phase rather than leaving it unscoped.
12. **Ship a marketing web page** even though the product is native-only — for SEO, for church
    bulletins, and for the App Store listing to link out to.
